02.01 / Leadership

Head Prefect

At Cheshire Academy, prefects are selected through interviews from more than 60 applicants, and only juniors and seniors may serve. I served as a dorm prefect in 2025–26, and this year I am one of two Head Prefects, a position reserved for seniors. I lead our prefect team, support boarding students, and serve as a bridge between students and faculty.

Much of my work involves mediation. I have helped roommates work through conflicts and, when necessary, collaborated with teachers to arrange room changes. One of my most challenging experiences came after racist comments were directed at Chinese students. I reported the incident to faculty, spoke with local students to better understand their perspectives, and shared those conversations with the affected students to help bridge misunderstandings while ensuring the incident was taken seriously.

I also serve on the Citizenship Committee, as one of 12 seniors selected from more than 40 applicants. Working alongside faculty, we review cases involving violations of school rules and help determine fair consequences. Both roles have taught me that leadership is less about authority than trust: people need to feel heard before rules can have meaning.

02.02 / Leadership

Head Ambassador

As Head Ambassador, I lead campus tours for prospective students and families and train new ambassadors to do the same. My goal on every tour is simple: show visitors not just our buildings, but what it actually feels like to live here.

I answer families honestly, share my own experience, and leave room for the questions they may feel shy to ask. I also introduce Cheshire to visiting international educational consultants and answer questions about academics, boarding life, and support for international students.

Last year, a student I had shown around decided to enroll, saying that my introduction, and seeing how happy I was here, made the difference. Helping someone else find a home here is the best way I know to share a school I love.

02.03 / Leadership

Writing Center Tutor

The Writing Center is staffed by just seven students, each selected through teacher recommendations. Our goal is not simply to turn a C into an A, but to help peers express their ideas more clearly, organize their arguments, and grow more confident in their writing.

I have tutored over 20 students in English and History essays. Every session starts with a question: What do you want to improve? Then we read the essay together. I point out gaps, such as a missing topic sentence or an argument that is hard to follow, and suggest ways to strengthen them. I want students to understand why a change might help, rather than simply follow my edits. The ideas, decisions, and final revisions are always theirs.

02.04 / Leadership

Calligraphy Club

I have practiced Chinese calligraphy since childhood, spending years learning to control the brush, shape each stroke, and appreciate the patience behind the art. What began as a personal passion became something I wanted to share with others. I founded a Calligraphy Club to introduce one of the most beautiful parts of Chinese culture to my school.

The club now has more than 30 members. Each month, we hold a practice session where members learn the basics, from holding the brush to building each stroke, before creating a final piece of their own choosing.

Our biggest event comes at Chinese New Year. We set up a table in the dining hall where any student can write 福 (fu), the character for good fortune, and hang it on their door. Practice paper lets first-time participants try a few strokes before writing their finished piece.

For many local students, this is their first hands-on encounter with Chinese culture. Calligraphy turns something unfamiliar into something they can hold, practice, and make their own. For me, it is a way to bring a piece of home to the place that has become my second one.

02.05 / Leadership

Assistant Coach

I spent thirteen years as a competitive figure skater until injuries made it impossible for me to continue training at the same level. Stepping off the ice as an athlete did not mean leaving the sport, so I returned to my former team as an assistant coach.

I worked with younger skaters, helping the head coach run practices by leading warm-ups, breaking down jumps and spins into smaller steps, and offering individual corrections. Having struggled through the same techniques myself, I understood how frustrating it could be when effort did not immediately lead to progress.

One young skater kept falling on a jump because she had become afraid of attempting it. Instead of asking her to try harder, I slowed everything down. We practiced the takeoff off-ice first, then moved onto the ice, where I supported her as she rebuilt her confidence. Within a few weeks, she landed the jump on her own. Watching her succeed reminded me that helping someone else move forward could be just as rewarding as landing a jump myself.

02.06 / Leadership

Volunteer Teaching

In the summer of 2023, I spent two weeks teaching English at a primary school in the mountains of Guangxi, China. My class had just over ten second graders, most of whom had no exposure to English.

I arrived with lesson plans built around vocabulary lists and daily conversation, but within the first day I could see they were not working. The children were shy, and memorizing words felt meaningless to them. I turned lessons into songs, games, and drawings, teaching colors, animals, and simple greetings through activities they could move and laugh through.

One boy barely spoke for the first few days. During a picture game, he suddenly shouted “elephant!” before anyone else, and the whole class cheered. After that, he raised his hand almost every lesson.

02.07 / Leadership

Cooking Club

As co-founder of the school's Cooking Club, I helped build a community of more than 60 students who share one belief: food is one of the easiest ways to bring people together.

During the year, members gather to make simple dishes and desserts, learning to cook side by side. For the International Food Festival, Mid-Autumn Festival, and Chinese New Year, we prepare food in large batches for students and teachers to taste.

For students far from home, these dishes are a taste of family. For everyone else, they are an invitation to understand where their friends come from.
